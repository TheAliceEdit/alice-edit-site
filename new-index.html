Alice H. I've made the mistakes so you don't have to.

You're not being dramatic for hesitating. A course that costs more than your rent deserves more than a countdown timer and a "doors close tonight" email.

Here's mine: I was selling trucks at a dealership when a woman in a pink G-Wagon showed up in my Instagram feed — matcha in hand, running her whole business from remote high-ticket sales. Then came the masterclasses, the price tags, and the promise: get placed on an offer, or get refunded.

I signed. I swiped. I showed up. When my turn came, the $12,000 offer I was placed on fizzled before it launched. I got swapped to a lower-ticket offer with no real support behind it either, and ended up closing for the course creator herself — no call reviews, no coaching, just unqualified leads. When I finally gave notice, I was met with silence.

None of that makes high-ticket sales a scam. I still think it's one of the best skills you can build. But the version sold on Instagram and the version that shows up after you pay can be two very different things — and that gap is exactly what this guide is for.

PART ONE

The five questions I wish I'd asked before I paid.

Not "is this a scam" questions. Quieter ones — the kind that are easy to skip when you're excited, and expensive to skip when you're not.

01
Do you actually relate to the person behind the program — or are you attracted to the life they're selling?
There's a difference between wanting someone's life and wanting them as your mentor.
02
What do their students' results REALLY look like?
A handful of incredible testimonials shows you what's possible. It doesn't show you what's typical.
03
What actually happens AFTER the course?
Finishing the modules isn't the finish line. It's when you start trying to use what you learned.
04
What are they actually promising you — and is it in writing?
The sales page, the enrollment call, and the contract should all tell you the same story.
05
Can I afford the investment AND the runway?
The price of the course isn't necessarily the full price of making the transition.
